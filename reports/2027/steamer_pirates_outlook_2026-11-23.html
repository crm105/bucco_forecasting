Pirates 2027: an elite rotation carrying a below-average lineup

Strengths

  • Starting rotation, 3rd in MLB. 15.1 WAR against an 11.0 average. Skenes (4.7), Ashcraft (3.8) and Jones (3.3) give it three top-tier starters. Starter FIP 3.80 ranks 5th.
  • Shortstop. Konnor Griffin is the best projected position player on the team (3.2 WAR, 108 wRC+, +5 fielding runs, +3 on the bases).
  • Center field and the bullpen. Cruz's center field ranks 10th and the bullpen 7th. Shortstop (12th) and catcher (17th) are about average.

Weaknesses

  • Run scoring. Offense ranks 25th, about 33 runs below the average team, or roughly 3 wins.
  • Defense at the bat-first spots. Defense ranks 26th. Reynolds (−12.5 runs in left), O'Hearn (−10.9) and Horwitz (−8.8) at first give back most of what their bats add.
  • Six of nine lineup spots rank 19th or worse at full playing time: third base 29th, second base 26th, DH 25th, left field 23rd, first base 20th and right field 19th. None of the projected regulars played mostly second base in 2026.

Steamer puts the Pirates second in the division, six wins behind Milwaukee. They have the Brewers' pitching but not their lineup.

What would move the number

The gap to an above-average team is almost all on the hitting side. At full playing time the lineup is 4.2 WAR below average. The biggest gaps are third base and second base (−0.9 each), left field (−0.8), and DH and first base (−0.6 each). Closing those would add about 4 wins. A regular DH bat matters more than it looks, because Pirates players cover less than half of the DH's playing time.

The rotation is the key risk. Skenes, Ashcraft and Jones are 11.8 of the 19.1 pitching WAR. A long absence from any of the three would take most of the staff's edge.

Third base

A third baseman would let Nick Gonzales move back to second, so the gain comes from the bench player who loses time (about 1.5 WAR per 600). Alec Bohm (PHI, 3B/1B; 107 wRC+, 2.1 WAR per 600) is worth about +0.6. Teruaki Sato, from Japan's NPB league (112 wRC+, 2.3 WAR per 600), projects a little better, but he has no MLB position history, so his projection is less certain.

What to target

A middle infielder (Chisholm, Arraez or Lowe, about +1 win), a corner-outfield bat (Suzuki or Arozarena, +1 to +1.3), and a starter (Rogers, +1.5) add up to roughly 3.5 to 4 WAR. That would take the projection from 81 to the mid-80s, within reach of Milwaukee's 87.

These figures don't include contract costs, ages or injury histories. Gains assume the new player takes all of one incumbent's playing time; in practice some of it would be shared. The list is from the November download, so check that each player is still unsigned.
