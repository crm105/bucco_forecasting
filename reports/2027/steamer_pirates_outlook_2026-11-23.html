Strengths

  • Starting rotation, 3rd in MLB. 15.1 WAR against an 11.3 average. Skenes (4.7), Ashcraft (3.8) and Jones (3.3) give it three top-tier starters. Starter FIP 3.80 ranks 5th.
  • Shortstop. Konnor Griffin is the team's most valuable position player: 3.5 WAR in 650 PA at short, with a 108 wRC+ and about +3 runs on the bases.
  • The bullpen. It ranks 11th, a little above average. Center field (16th) is about average.

Weaknesses

  • Run scoring. Batting ranks 27th, about 39 runs below the average team's lineup, or roughly 4 wins.
  • Defense. Fielding plus positional adjustment ranks 23rd. The weakest gloves in the lineup are Reynolds in left (−4.4 fielding runs) and Gonzales at third (−3.2).
  • Five of nine lineup spots rank 21st or worse: first base and third base 28th, left field 23rd, second base 22nd and catcher 21st. None of the projected regulars played mostly second base in 2026.

Positions against the league

Every team fields every position for a full season: 640 PA at catcher and 700 at each other spot, DH included. Our lineup optimizer decides who plays where on each team to get the most batting WAR. It accounts for fatigue (hitters do a little worse the more they play) and for how a fielder's defense changes when he moves position, measured from Statcast. Time that no rostered player can fill better than a replacement-level player goes to one, at 0 WAR. Pitching is Steamer's own projection: the rotation is pitchers who start at least half their games.

The forecast puts the Pirates second in the division, about eight wins behind Milwaukee. They have the Brewers' pitching but not their lineup.

What would move the number

The gap to an above-average team is almost all on the hitting side. The lineup is 4.4 WAR below average. The biggest gaps are third base (−1.1), first base (−0.9) and left field (−0.8), then second base (−0.5) and catcher and DH (−0.4 each). Closing them would add about 4 wins.

The rotation is the key risk. Skenes, Ashcraft and Jones are 11.8 of the 19.1 pitching WAR. A long absence from any of the three would take most of the staff's edge.

Suzuki's third-base time is a model quirk: Statcast's fielding measure doesn't count outfield arms, so it rates third base as only slightly harder than right field. Read his gain as that of a corner bat. Teruaki Sato (Japan's NPB league) has no MLB id in the file and isn't included.

The pattern is clear. The two corner bats with 120+ wRC+ (Suzuki, Arozarena) are worth about 2 wins each, because they push the weaker bats (O'Hearn, Horwitz, Gonzalez) out of the DH, first base and corner spots. A second baseman (Arraez, Chisholm or Lowe) is worth about 1.3 to 1.6. Signing one of each is close to additive: Suzuki and Arraez together add 3.5 WAR, Arozarena and Lowe 3.0.

What to target

A corner bat (Suzuki or Arozarena) and a second baseman (Arraez or Lowe) together add about 3.0 to 3.5 WAR. A starter like Rogers adds about 1.5 more. That is roughly 4.5 to 5 WAR in all, which at about one win per WAR would take the forecast from 81 to about 86, within two or three wins of Milwaukee.

These figures don't include contract costs, ages or injury histories. Batting gains come from re-optimizing the lineup; pitching gains compare the free agent with the weakest starter he would replace. The list is from the November download, so check that each player is still unsigned.
